## The scheme (as built)
Per cell: S = Σ ν_r ω_r, J = Σ ν_r ∇ω_r (∇ω_r from `Omega::omega` with X_j := 1, no change to rates.rs), solve (I − hJ)ΔX = hS (10×10 Gauss, partial pivoting), Δξ_r = h(ω_r + ∇ω_r·ΔX), heat q_r Δξ_r into e_tot and the group's accumulator each accepted sub-step.
h = min(t_rem, burn_dx/max|S|, burn_dt·T/|dT/dt|, 2 × the last h); the accepted step is checked (ΔX ≤ burn_dx, ΔT/T ≤ burn_dt, X ≥ 0 within 1e-12) and halved on failure; clamp ≥ 0, renormalise. MAX_ATTEMPTS 1,000,000 per cell per pass (then `unfinished`).
Energy: E += Σ·Σ q Δξ; ε_th = e_tot − ε_cold(new Y_e, X_n), floored at T_floor/μ for the rates only. pin_temperature: T held at the start for the rates, E rewritten with ε_th = T/μ(new), heat still booked.

3. pin_temperature keeps T (not E) across steps.

## For M0-T46 (GPU) — measured, not acted on
- f32 will not hold 1e-4 over ~10⁴ sub-steps: the GPU cases need a measured f32 bound (a D or a contract question, never a looser number silently), or a higher-order sub-step.
- The tight-burn_dx case costs ~9,000 sub-steps for one cell; a hot core against a cold field is the divergent-loop cost the block's adversarial asks about.
- A retry loop (halving) exists because the implicit step is not bound by its rate predictor; the shader can keep the predictor only and clamp, at the price of the guarantee.
