## How the ratio is read
From the mass centroid, 360 rays; along each ray the first crossing of a level (bilinear between cell centres, 0.05-cell march).
`ax/dg` = mean radius on the four axes / mean on the four diagonals (a perfect square 0.707, a diamond 1.414, round 1.00);
`a4` = the cos 4θ amplitude of r(θ) over r̄. Σ levels are relative to the central peak or absolute (Σ_vac = 1e-6 → 1.5e-6 read).
The temperature proxy `eint` = (E − ½Σ|u|²)/Σ.

## P4's square is the scheme's, not the GPU's, and does not converge away
- **Twin = GPU.** A hydro-only Maclaurin disk (a = 24, Σc = 1, T = 20, 128², leave) on the f64 twin (`--cpu-reference`, linux-pc CPU)
  and on the RTX 4090: the edge 0.9406 on both, Σ 1e-4 0.9447 on both, to four digits at steps 100–400.
- **Resolution** (hydro-only, the same disk scaled, 600 × 400, read at the same physical time, front at r ≈ 1.9a): edge 0.952 (a 12) ·
  0.941 (a 24) · 0.944 (a 48) · 0.953 (a 96); the Σ 0.01 contour behind it converges to round — 0.964 · 0.996 · 0.992 · 0.999.
- **The floors are not it**: Σ_vac 1e-10 and Σ_floor 1e-12 (overrides, 100× lower) move the edge 0.941 → 0.950.
- **The mechanism, measured**: at Σ = 1e-4 the gas on the diagonals moves faster — a 24: 20.1 against 16.3 on the axes (× 1.23); a 96:
  20.8 against 19.8 — the velocity purely radial; at Σ = 1e-2 the two match (15.5 / 15.4). Each face solves a one-dimensional
  problem against vacuum (HLL, §2.3.4), which drives the face-normal velocity toward the escape speed; a cell on a diagonal is driven
  by an x-face and a y-face at once, so its thin gas outruns the axes' (× √2 is the fully per-axis limit). This is a known property
  of face-by-face (dimension-wise) Riemann fluxes at a vacuum front; it lives where Σ is a few orders below the star's.
