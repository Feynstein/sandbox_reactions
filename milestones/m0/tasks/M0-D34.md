## The cause, measured

Lockstep (a temporary GPU module, removed): each step, the GPU's state before the step went through the twin's P5 + P6
with the GPU's own Δt, and the result was compared with the GPU's state after the step, cell (32, 32) of ref_burn.json.

- Steps 0–43: P6 agrees each step to ≤ 5 × 10⁻⁵ relative in ΔE (f32); the twin's ΔE equals its booked nuclear exactly.
- Step 44 is a runaway: every cell burns H → … → Fe in one step (275 sub-steps in the twin, T to ≈ 18,800). ΔE GPU
  2.6079e5, twin 2.5845e5: **9.0 × 10⁻³**. The twin is insensitive to its input (±6e-8 … 3e-7 on E: ΔE unchanged to 7
  digits), so the gap is the GPU's path.
- Cause 1 (P6): `LN_F_MAX = 60` in react.wgsl clips each record's ln f; the power law reaches e^194 (He, ν 40, T/T_He ≈
  128). Clipping each record flattens the ratios between competing records (He_burn against C_alpha), so the He group is
  booked 0.44 % apart (36,158 against 36,001 per cell) while every other group agrees within 10⁻⁷. With the twin clipped
  the same way (a temporary env-gated cap in rates.rs, removed): step 44 agrees to **5.4 × 10⁻⁸**, and over 200 steps on a
  heat-off copy of ref_burn.json E L1 is **6.3 × 10⁻⁸** (as found: 8.5 × 10⁻³ — T48's heat-off number).
- Cause 2 (P5, not P6): from step 45 the field is no longer uniform (edge cells ignite later); P5 needs 35–549 RKL2
  stages on steps 45–54 and the GPU caps at 32 (contract §0.5 [M0-T42]); the twin has no cap. Exactly those steps disagree
  (ΔE up to 100 % per step); from step 55 (≤ 32 stages) the two agree to ~10⁻⁵. With P6 matched (twin clipped), the full
  ref_burn.json still reads E L1 **2.83 × 10⁻²** and sim time −2.86 %. The block's premise "P5 is not the cause" held only
  for heat-off: with heat on, P5's cap is the larger part of T48's 3.07 × 10⁻².
- Adversarial: per side, the energy booked equals ΔE (twin exactly; the GPU's groups track the twin's to 10⁻⁷ outside the
  He group), so neither side books energy without its q·Δm.

## The GPU rate scale (built and measured here, not in the tree — tasks/M0-D34.react-scale.patch)

The patch applies to react.wgsl as found (`git apply --check` passes). What it does, and what it measured:

- **Scale, not clip:** per sub-step, shift = max(0, largest ln f over *burning* records (every input present) − 60);
  every ω, ∇ω, S, Σqω and ε_ν is taken e^−shift times the true one, and h runs in that scaled time (true h = h e^−shift,
  time left t_rem e^shift, +inf past f32 — then it bounds nothing, as in the twin's f64). shift = 0 leaves the old
  arithmetic. A record live by its gradient alone (one order-1 input absent) is held to e^80 in that scale (h|∂ω/∂X| ≫ 1
  there: backward Euler's limit). The latch reads the true sign.
- **Divided solve:** where h·max|J| > 10³⁰, solve (I/h − J) ΔX = S instead of (I − hJ) ΔX = hS (measured: h·J ≈ 10⁵³ at
  sub-step 171 of step 44, NaN in f32).
- **h_try** carried across a change of shift (h_try · e^(shift − shift_try)), floored at f32's smallest normal as the twin
  floors at f64::MIN_POSITIVE (an underflow to 0 then 0 · inf was a NaN).
- **Negative margin** −max(1e-12, 2.4e-7 |ΔX|) instead of −1e-7 flat: the flat margin accepted an overshoot of C to 0 at
  sub-step 54 where the twin halved once more; with it, retries match the twin (103,792 against 103,800 over 4,096 cells).
- Measured with the power law (before the ruling): step 44 9.0e-3 → **6.1e-4**; heat-off ref_burn 200 steps E L1
  **5.7e-4**. Sub-step traces agree to ~10⁻⁵ through sub-step 133; past it trace He (≈10⁻¹³, its X³ flushed to zero as
  subnormal) and C (≈10⁻²⁵) under e^156 rate constants drive f32 elsewhere. **Still open:** with heat on, an edge cell
  igniting later (x 21, y 0) reaches T ≈ 47,000, shift 173, S ≈ 2e-38 beside a gradient ≈ 1e26: h ≈ 5e34 and h·g·ΔX
  overflow in the heat sum → non-finite energy at step 64. The shift is taken from ln f alone, so a trace species with a
  huge f sets it; under the Gamow law the spread between records shrinks (He ≈ e^95 against Si ≈ e^63 at T 18,000, against
  e^194 / e^113 now), which is where M0-D35 should start.
