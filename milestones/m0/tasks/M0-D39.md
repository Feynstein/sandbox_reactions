# M0-D39 — timing's 1000-step frame at SLOTS rounds: the cause, and a frame the box survives

Run on linux-pc (laserax-ai), RTX 5090 (Vulkan), 2026-10-15; the lead was on linux-pc, so the block's win-laptop Verify is
owed there (R5, R15). Raw output: logs/M0-D39.log (probes), logs/M0-D39.timing.log, logs/M0-D39-base.timing.log.

## Measured (the cause)

- At `SLOTS` (4,290) recorded rounds, P5 costs **9.84 ms a step** on the RTX 5090 on the 128 × 128 disk (GPU timestamps,
  the case's own frame cost; the whole step 9.997 ms). That is ~2.3 µs for each recorded `heat_round`, most of them
  zero-sized. So the case's **1000-step frame is ~10 s of GPU work in one submission**. That is five times Windows' 2 s
  GPU timeout (TDR), which fits the RTX 4080 Laptop's "Parent device is lost" at 106 s (M0-D37). NVIDIA on Linux has no
  such watchdog here, so linux-pc stayed GO.
- The CPU side on linux-pc: encoding the 4.29 M dispatches took 34–56 s and `queue.submit` 10.5–11.2 s (the GPU's ~10 s
  runs inside it); the wait after it was 0.05–0.07 s. That case alone was 45–72 s of the scope's 66 s (load 15–27).
- NOT PROVEN (source only): whether M0-D37's 16-pass hang past 900 s was the same reset, unreported. Win-laptop's
  slower CPU over 4.2 M dispatches fits it too. Not measured on win-laptop from here.
- Slots each step uses on the fresh 128 × 128 disk (one step a submission at `SLOTS`, rounds + 3 × passes + 2, RTX 5090):
  steps 0–335 use at most 83 (79 at step 2, 83 at step 319). The heating front arrives at step 336 (222), 339 (339), and
  peaks at step 346 (1,627). Steps 380–419 use 205–636.

## Built

- crates/sr-engine/tests/gpu/timing.rs, `a_frame_longer_than_the_query_set_times_its_first_whole_steps`: the frame is
  `FRAME` = 300 steps at `ROUNDS` = 256 fixed rounds (was 1000 at `SLOTS`).
  - It still outruns the query set: ≈ 255 steps fit, 255 timed, 300 ended. A new guard, `fits + 20 < FRAME`, fails if
    the pass list grows until the frame no longer outruns it (the block's adversarial line).
  - Rounds stay fixed, as the lead ruled at M0-T121 («Fix rounds in that case»). 256 is 3× the most these steps use, so
    none carries, and `steps_run == FRAME` asserts it.
  - The new submission: 0.758 ms a step × 300 ≈ 0.23 s on the RTX 5090. The `SLOTS` import is dropped.
- tools/pb/verify.json, timing's `desc`: "a 300-step frame …", its rounds fixed and its one submission inside a 2 s GPU
  timeout. Expected count unchanged (5).

## Deviations (declared)

- The rounds are fixed at 256, not `SLOTS`. M0-T121 chose `SLOTS` so no step could carry. The 300-step frame never
  reaches the front, so 256 never carries either; if a step did carry, `steps_run == FRAME` would fail.
- The block's Done-when wants timing GO on win-laptop too. The lead was on linux-pc, so Verify 1 is owed on win-laptop
  (R15) and flagged to M0-V6. The RTX 4080 Laptop's frame time is NOT PROVEN.

## Measured, not acted on

- Each zero-sized indirect `heat_round` costs ~2.3 µs of GPU on the RTX 5090 (4.9 µs on the Quadro, M0-T119's flag).
  A caller or engine choice near `SLOTS` therefore costs ~10 ms a step on any frame. `Step::run` and frames at the
  engine's learnt choice record far fewer rounds, and P5's cost was accepted at M0-T122. Not filed: no M0 path records
  `SLOTS` rounds over many steps now. A future frame over 2 s of GPU on Windows would hit the same TDR.
