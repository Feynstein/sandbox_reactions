## How the latch reaches every dispatch
Under `encode_frame`, every dispatch of a step takes its workgroup count from a buffer the controller can zero:
- a fixed-size dispatch from its slot in `latch.args` — one slot per (size, schedule); the guard's slots (period 64)
  and the re-fit's (period 16) are opened by `latch_begin`/`latch_check` from the Δt record's step count (the GPU's step
  index), so the guard and the re-fit are recorded on every step of a frame and run only on their schedule;
- a box-sized dispatch from `box.args` (saved, zeroed, restored by `latch_end`);
- P0's re-fit (`box_cells`, `box_fit` on the gate) from `box.gate` (zeroed, restored the same way).
`Latch::new` asserts the step reads exactly those two indirect buffers, so a later pass with its own indirect sizes
(P7, M0-T54) fails loudly until latch.wgsl zeroes it too. The controller itself and `latch_begin`/`latch_end` keep their
own fixed size: they run whether or not the latch fired.

## Rule as built (my reading of §1.8.5)
`since` = sim time since the last set, Δt of the step added before the test, held at 1.0 (a new latch starts at 1.0:
armed). A set with `since ≥ 1.0` and the slow-down on fires; every set — fired, held, or with the slow-down off —
restarts `since` at 0. The flag is taken by the controller each step (atomicExchange).

## Deviations
1. `encode_frame` and `poll` are methods of `Step` (there is no `Engine` yet, §3.1 names the meanings, not the types);
   `poll` takes the device (it calls `device.poll(PollType::Poll)`, never a wait). The slow-down setting rides in
   `FramePlan` and reaches the GPU as a buffer copy, since `encode_frame` has no queue.
2. Under frames the guard's and the re-fit's schedule is the GPU's step index; bare `encode`/`run` keeps the CPU count.
   After a frame the latch stopped, the CPU count runs ahead of the GPU's, so a run uses frames or bare steps, not both
   (documented on `Step::encode`). headless.rs still uses bare `run` — unchanged.
3. `poll()`'s report has no cost per step yet — GPU timestamps are M0-T23's (flagged there).
4. Readbacks: one 128-byte MAP_READ buffer per frame in flight (latch record + Δt record), mapped by
   `map_buffer_on_submit`; a frame encoded but never submitted keeps its buffer busy (a new one is made for the next).
